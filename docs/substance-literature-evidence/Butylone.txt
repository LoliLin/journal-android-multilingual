=== Translational Toxicology of N-Isopropylbutylone: Integrated In Vivo and In Silico Assessment of a New Synthetic Cathinone
    Bassi M, Bilel S, Corli G, Cocita N, Masoli A, Bernardi T, Odoardi S, Mestria S, Biosa G, Strano-Rossi S, Marti M.
    ? 2026 | PMID - | PMCID PMC13665128 | DOI -
    https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/13665128
    - Predicted metabolism of N -isopropylbutylone (NiPB) by ADMET Predictor ® .
    - The in silico analysis revealed a metabolic profile consistent with previous computational, in vitro, and in vivo studies on related cathinones [ 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 ]. Indeed, NiPB was predicted to undergo N-dealkylation, producing a metabolite shared with butylone and N-propylbutylone. A second predicted metabolite results from cleavage of the 3,4-methylendioxy moiety. These reactions were predicted to be mediated by CYP1A2, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6. Furthermore, in silico modelling suggested a potential for CYP2D6 inhibition, although this output was generated with moderate prediction confidence (accuracy of 58%), representing a hypothetical risk for pharmacokinetic drug–drug interactions that requires experimental validation. While future studies are needed to confirm these pathways, khat has been reported to inhibit CYP2D6 in humans [ 15 ], and an in vitro study demonstrated the ability of methylenedioxy-derived designer drugs, butylone and MDPV, to inhibit this isoform [ 16 ]. Furthermore, eutylone was recently shown to be metabolized by CYP2D6, CYP2C19, and CYP2B6 while inducing a time-dependent inhibition of CYP2D6 [ 17 ]. Finally, the software predicted phase II N-glucuronidation by UGT1A4 and UGT2B7. Although information regarding phase II metabolites of synthetic cathinones remains limited, glucuronide conjugates have been identified for both eutylone and mephedrone [ 11 , 17 , 18 ].

=== In vitro metabolic profiling of methylenedioxy-substituted synthetic cathinones for enhanced detection in urine sample analysis.
    Yeh YL, Hsieh CL, Huang YJ, Chang YH, Wang SM.
    ? 2025 | PMID 40592338 | DOI 10.38212/2224-6614.3543
    - This study investigated the in vitro metabolism of nine methylenedioxy-substituted synthetic cathinones, including methylone and eight structural isomers, which were categorized into three groups: Group 1 (dimethylone, ethylone, and butylone), Group 2 (dibutylone, pentylone, and eutylone), and Group 3 (N-ethylpentylone and dipentylone).
    - Key pathways included N-dealkylation, β-ketone reduction, aliphatic hydroxylation, demethylenation, and glucuronide conjugation, with the analytes containing N, N-dimethylamino moieties showing higher N-dealkylation and β-ketone reduction ratios.
    - Overall, this study provides a unified comparison of the products of synthetic cathinone metabolism, and highlights the importance of metabolites as key markers for enhanced identification accuracy in forensic toxicology.

=== Enantioselectivity of Pentedrone and Methylone on Metabolic Profiling in 2D and 3D Human Hepatocyte-like Cells.
    Silva B, Rodrigues JS, Almeida AS, Lima AR, Fernandes C, Guedes de Pinho P, Miranda JP, Remião F.
    ? 2022 | PMID 35337165 | PMCID PMC8953427 | DOI 10.3390/ph15030368
    https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/8953427
    - Despite their importance, enantioselectivity studies still constitute a significant challenge for the research community, owing to the difficulties in enantiomer isolation and to the lack of data in relevant in vitro models. Liver’s susceptibility to the toxicity of synthetic cathinones has already been proved by clinical data [ 1 , 9 ] and in vitro studies for different cathinone derivatives, namely for pentedrone, methylone, 4-MEC [ 10 ], 3,4-methylenedioxypyrovalerone (MDPV) [ 10 , 28 ], butylone, buphedrone, and 3,4-DMMC [ 29 ] in PRH. However, the enantioselectivity of these compounds has not yet been studied in the liver. Thus, the present work aimed to unravel the enantioselectivity metabolic and cytotoxic profile of pentedrone and methylone by resorting to two in vitro hepatic models, HLCs cultured in 2D and 3D. Herein, it is shown that the estimated EC 50 values for both pentedrone and methylone are distinct in both models, being higher in 3D cultures than in 2D cultures. Most importantly, the enantioselectivity metabolic profile of pentedrone and methylone in 2D- and 3D-HLCs is demonstrated for the first time. Finally, it is further shown that enantioselective cytotoxicity was only observed in the 3D model, where the R -(−)-pentedrone, which is preferentially metabolized over the S enantiomer, is also the most toxic enantiomer in this cellular model.
