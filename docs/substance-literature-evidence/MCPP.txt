=== Characterization of trazodone metabolic pathways and species-specific profiles.

    ? 2025 | PMID 41098834 | DOI 10.3389/fphar.2025.1636919
    -  Background Trazodone, an antidepressant indicated for the treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD), undergoes complex metabolism involving multiple cytochrome P450 (CYP) isoforms.
    - CYP isoforms involved in trazodone metabolism were identified through selective CYP inhibitors and recombinant enzymes.
    - CYP3A4 was identified as the primary enzyme mediating trazodone metabolism, particularly in the formation of mCPP while CYP2D6, CYP2C19, and FMOs contributed to the formation of other major, inactive metabolites such as M9 and M2.

=== The advent of molecular nanocarbon biology.
    Amaike K, Ueda A, Itami K.
    ? 2026 | PMID 42666526 | PMCID PMC13522950 | DOI 10.1039/d6sc05731d
    https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/13522950
    - As an emerging class of cyclic molecular nanocarbons for biological applications, Itami, Amaike, Yagi, and coworkers recently reported the first imaging probe based on the aromatic belt, methylene-bridged cycloparaphenylene (MCPP), and elucidated its unique cellular internalization behavior in mammalian cells ( Fig. 3D ). 41 Owing to the relatively high acidity of the methylene hydrogen atoms, direct functionalization of MCPP is feasible. Treatment of MCPP with propargyl bromide under basic conditions enabled the one-step synthesis of a clickable MCPP derivative. Subsequent conjugation with fluorescein afforded a fluorescent probe, whose cellular uptake behavior was investigated in detail. Interestingly, the molecule initially accumulated on the cell membrane before being internalized. Experimental and computational studies suggested that the interior cavity of the belt structure interacts with membrane lipids or membrane-associated proteins, thereby influencing its membrane affinity and uptake pathway.
