    - 3,4-Methylenedioxyethylamphetamine is the N-ethyl analogue of MDMA. In humans it undergoes O-demethylenation to 3,4-dihydroxyethylamphetamine (DHEA), followed by O-methylation to 4-hydroxy-3-methoxyethylamphetamine (HMEA) and conjugation; a minor N-deethylation pathway yields MDA. Detection of HMEA in urine or blood supports recent MDEA exposure and complements MDMA: MDA-based timing. Confirmation by targeted liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry is recommended when source attribution is disputed [ 140 , 142 ]. Therefore, MDEA biomarkers, though informative, must always be interpreted in a broader metabolic and analytical context.

Although amine-based solvents such as methyl diethanolamine (MDEA) exhibit high CO 2 absorption capacity and bioavailable CO 2 release without forming a strong covalent bond, their potential biotoxicity remains a key concern. , A recent study demonstrated that methanogens within mixed microbial consortia exhibit varying responses to different capture agents due to their biocidal properties, , which can differentially inhibit methanogenesis. Among several capture agents tested, including monoethanolamine (MEA), polyethylene glycol (PEG-200), and N 1 -dodecyl- N 3 , N 3 -dimethylpropane-1,3-diamine (C 12 -DMAPA), MDEA was identified as a promising candidate for BICCU. This limitation poses a challenge for industrial scalability, as conventional amine scrubbing operates at capture solvent concentrations of 1.5–2.5 mol/L.

In contrast, the downregulation of lysine biosynthesis and secretion system genes at 100 mmol/L MDEA suggested an energy trade-off prioritizing stress responses over energy metabolism and growth of the methanogen.
    - MDEA may interfere with methanogenesis by inhibiting key enzymes. The differential expression of genes involved in coenzyme biosynthesis, including coenzyme B, F 420 , and cobalamin, suggests that MDEA exposure disrupts the synthesis or function of essential cofactors required for methane metabolism. Such inhibition would significantly impact the efficiency of methanogenesis, as these cofactors play vital roles in electron transfer and enzymatic catalysis within the methanogenesis pathway. , The upregulation of genes associated with cobalt/nickel transport under MDEA-induced stress may indicate a cellular response to maintain or increase the availability of these metal ions, which are required for the biosynthesis of several methanogenic cofactors, given the established role of cobalt and nickel uptake systems in supporting cofactor biosynthesis in methanogens.
