=== Biosynthesis of benzylisoquinoline alkaloids and its evolution in plants.
    Tian Y, An Z, Li Q, Bai G, Zhang Q, Dong Y, Chen Y, Chen S, Xu Z, Gao R.
    ? 2026 | PMID 41776991 | PMCID PMC12983256 | DOI 10.1016/j.xplc.2026.101786
    https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/12983256
    - The biosynthesis of BIAs begins with a conserved upstream pathway that converts L -tyrosine into the key intermediate ( S )-reticuline. Utilizing ( S )-reticuline and ( S )- N -methylcoclaurine as precursors, various CYP450 subfamilies (including members of the CYP80A/B/G/Q, CYP719B, and CYP71BG) and flavin-dependent oxidases (e.g., BBE) catalyze regio- and stereospecific intramolecular or intermolecular phenolic couplings. These reactions lead to the formation of distinct BIA structural scaffolds, including aporphines, BBRs, BZPs, PQs, morphinans, protopines, and bisBIAs.
    - ( S )-Methylcanadine, generated from ( S )-scoulerine by 9OMT, CYP719A, and tetrahydroprotoberberine N -methyltransferase (TNMT), is the biosynthetic precursor for PQs, protopines, and BZPs. ( S )- cis - N -methylstylopine 14-hydroxylase (CYP82N4) catalyzes the conversion of ( S )-methylcanadine and ( S )-methylstylopine into protopine-type alkaloids, which are subsequently transformed into BZPs by protopine 6-hydroxylase (CYP82N3) ( Beaudoin and Facchini, 2013 ). Meanwhile, ( S )-methylcanadine undergoes a series of enzymatic modifications, including hydroxylation by CYP450s (CYP82X1, CYP82X2, and CYP82Y1), acetylation by acetyltransferase (AT1), and ester hydrolysis by carboxylesterase (CXE) to form PQ-type alkaloids ( Li et al., 2018 ).
    - BBE and BBEL enzymes play an indispensable role in expanding the structural diversity of BIAs, particularly BBR and BZP types, through their unique two- or four-electron oxidative reactivities. However, the heterologous expression of functional BBE enzymes remains challenging, primarily due to poor solubility, incorrect folding, and inefficient incorporation of the FAD cofactor. These limitations have significantly hindered the reconstruction of efficient BIA biosynthetic pathways in microbial or plant systems. Advanced protein-engineering strategies, such as directed evolution and rational design, hold great potential to overcome these barriers and enable efficient synthetic production of high-value BIAs.
