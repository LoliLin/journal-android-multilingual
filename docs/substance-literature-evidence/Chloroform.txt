    - Historically, N- demethylation has been performed by synthetic chemistry, using harsh and unsustainable reagents, such as cyanogen bromide, via the von Braun reaction, as well as chloroformates [ 6 , 8 ]. Biocatalysis has the potential to provide greener methods for industrial production [ 9 ], but so far this approach has only been explored to a limited extent for demethylation. One study describes the discovery of an N- demethylase from Methylobacterium that when expressed in E. coli and purified could be used to N- demethylate thebaine. The enzyme exhibited broad substrate specificity, with good to complete yields of N- demethylated products. Maximum product titres were very low, however, ranging from 2.0 µM to 7.5 µM for various isoquinoline, tropane, indole and piperidine alkaloids [ 6 ].

This novel methodology follows the aziridination-ring opening arrangement to insert a single nitrogen atom.
