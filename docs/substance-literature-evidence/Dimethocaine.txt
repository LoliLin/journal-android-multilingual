=== Contribution of human esterases to the metabolism of selected drugs of abuse.
    Meyer MR, Schütz A, Maurer HH.
    ? 2015 | PMID 25445008 | DOI 10.1016/j.toxlet.2014.10.026
    - Human esterases such as the human carboxylesterases (hCES) are important for the catalytic ester hydrolysis of xenobiotics and they play an important role in the detoxification of drugs (e.g., cocaine) but also in the activation of prodrugs (e.g., ramipril).
    - Therefore, the aim of the presented study was to characterize the enzyme-catalyzed ester hydrolysis of ten drugs (cocaine, dimethocaine, ethylphenidate, 4-fluoro-3α-tropacocaine, 4-fluoro-3β-tropacocaine, heroin, methylphenidate, mitragynine, ramipril, and thebacon) by different esterase-containing systems (recombinant hCES1b, hCES1c, and hCES2, pooled human liver microsomes, pooled human liver S9 fraction, and pooled human plasma).
    - The hydrolysis reactions followed classic Michaelis-Menten kinetics with exception of cocaine and 4-fluoro-3α-tropacocaine, for which hydrolysis rate was too low for reliable modeling.
